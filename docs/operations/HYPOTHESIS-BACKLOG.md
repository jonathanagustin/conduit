| PH-6 ([#13](https://github.com/jonathanagustin/conduit/issues/13)) | Found while regrounding the corpus: the CEL examples page publishes `require_operation(rid, "entity:approve-config-update", ...)`. | An upstream-authored CEL policy using a published operation name is satisfiable here. | **REJECT as proposed; QUEUED as re-scoped.** The hypothesis holds: an Apollo-authored policy naming `entity:approve-config-update` matches nothing here, so the change is unapprovable and nothing says why. The *reasoning* recorded here was wrong, and it was wrong in the direction that would have caused damage. It said the operation "matches no grant and no role" and concluded the vocabulary should be closed and validated like the Change Type taxonomy. It does match a grant: `RequirementSatisfied` compares `requirement.Operation` against `ResourceGrant.Operations`, which `PutGrant` deliberately does not constrain, so an operator-defined approval token is a working feature — `TestCELRoleAndOperationRequirementsUseResourceGrants` has exercised `environment:approve-change` end to end since before this row was written. The operation constants are what Platform *performs*; grant operations are what a policy may *demand*, and the two namespaces only share a field. Re-scoped: the real defect is that a requirement nothing can satisfy stays pending forever instead of being reported, which is a question about grants at evaluation time, not about a vocabulary at authoring time. |
